## C. Project description (the long page)

### 1. The one-sentence version
The Dry Lightning 3000 is a gas-engine high-pressure water pump you can start, run up to full throttle, open the water valve on, and shut down from a handheld remote, so you can wet down your home, barn or defensible space without standing at the pump.

### 2. Why I built this
[REVIEW: Jeremy must confirm or rewrite this section in his own voice. The records contain no first-person origin story; the facts below are all that is documented. Do not add a personal fire-loss story unless it is true.]

I'm Jeremy Coon. I live and work in Bend, Oregon, in the middle of the country's wildland-urban interface. For the past two years I have been building remote-control systems for water pumps in my shop: first wireless sprinkler controllers in a toolbox, then a fixed three-building system I installed on the Metolius River in spring 2026, and then the question that turned into this product.

Every gas fire pump on the market assumes somebody is standing next to it. In a real fire, that somebody has evacuated, is asleep, or does not want to walk toward the embers to pull a rope. I wanted a pump I could start from the truck, the porch, or the end of the driveway, and shut down the same way.

So I designed the control system around a commercial Honda engine: relays that turn the ignition, starter, throttle and a powered discharge valve into four buttons on a rolling-code key fob, wired so that if the remote link or the power ever drops, the engine kills, the throttle returns to idle and the valve closes on its own. I built the prototype, filed two provisional patent applications on the automatic version in January 2026, and by August 2026 had the unit you see in these photos starting and stopping wirelessly from my phone and from a bench switch. The key-fob control box is the production design of that same relay system; building and testing it on this pump is the first job this campaign funds, and it will be on video before launch.

This campaign funds the first production batch of the fob-controlled version, built and tested in Bend.

### 3. The problem, plainly
- Insurers in wildfire areas are non-renewing policies and pricing mitigation into rates. (Background sources, not statistics we claim: California Department of Insurance 2026 briefing; Oregon Division of Financial Regulation 2026 emergency order on non-renewals; reporting on Deschutes County FAIR Plan growth. See `01_research/kickstarter_rules_and_competitors.md` §6.)
- Installed exterior sprinkler systems cost in the tens of thousands of dollars and take a contractor.
- A good portable pump costs a thousand to two thousand dollars and needs a person at the pull-cord, the choke and the valve.
- Add-on engine remote-start kits exist, but they are start/stop only. They do not run the throttle up, and they do not open a water valve.

### 4. What the Dry Lightning 3000 does
| Button | What happens | Status (2026-09-17) |
|---|---|---|
| A — Crank | Starter engages for a timed pulse | Remote crank demonstrated on the prototype (wireless ESP link); fob receiver wiring designed, to be built and filmed before launch |
| B — Run / Stop | Ignition enabled; press again to stop | Remote run/kill demonstrated on the prototype; fob path as above |
| C — Throttle | Idle ↔ full throttle | Throttle receiver built in the prototype; final polarity test pending; fob path as above |
| D — Valve | 2 in. discharge valve closed ↔ open | Motorized valve controlled in the prototype; spring-return failsafe actuator is the production part, not yet fitted |

[REVIEW: Kickstarter requires the prototype to perform every advertised function on camera. The fob-controlled build must exist and be filmed before this page is submitted. Until then this section is a development statement, not a demonstration claim.]

Safety interlock: buttons C and D are physically dead unless the engine is enabled to run. Press B to stop and both the throttle and the valve drop to their safe default automatically. Loss of power or remote signal does the same. Manual switches on the pump work with nothing plugged in.

Range: rolling-code RF, roughly 150 ft line of sight [REVIEW: not yet measured on the production receivers; publish the measured figure from the range test or say "measured before shipping"].

### 7. How it fits into your property
A pump is only one part of watering a property. You supply the water (pond, tank, pool, cistern, irrigation line), the suction and discharge hoses, and the nozzle or sprinklers. The DL3000 moves the water and lets you control the engine and valve from a distance. Typical uses: wetting roofs, decks and defensible space ahead of an approaching fire, filling tanks, feeding existing sprinkler lines, and general high-pressure water transfer on ranches and camps.

### 8. What this campaign does NOT promise
- It is not a monitoring, alarm or fire-detection device. It does not start itself.
- It does not connect to the internet or a phone in v1.
- It does not include sprinklers or installation.
- It does not make property fireproof, does not guarantee protection, and does not replace defensible space, firefighters or evacuation orders. If you are told to evacuate, evacuate.
- It is preparedness equipment. It requires an operator present to supervise it, a water source, fuel and hoses.

### 9. Phone control: where it really stands
The prototype has a second control path: an ESP-based wireless link to a self-hosted dashboard that I have used to start and stop the pump from my phone (first end-to-end cloud start on 2026-08-23; a short demo was recorded). It works in my shop. It is not in the v1 reward because:
1. A custom radio design needs its own FCC certification, which costs far more than the declaration path used for the pre-certified fob receivers.
2. The product-liability insurance being placed for v1 is scoped to a locally-operated pump with no app, internet or monitoring.
3. Phone control depends on a power source, a connectivity path at the pump, and water and hoses being already in place, and I am not going to sell "start it from anywhere" until the failure modes are engineered and tested.

Every DL3000 ships with the documented Remote Port so a future connected controller can plug in without rewiring. If this campaign funds, part of the surplus goes to that development. It is a development goal, not a stretch reward.

## E. FAQ (22 questions)

1. **Is this a sprinkler system?** No. It is a pump with remote engine and valve control. You connect your own hoses, nozzle or sprinkler lines.
2. **Does it start by itself when it detects fire?** No. Version 1 starts only when you press the fob. Automatic and phone-triggered operation exist as a prototype and are a future development goal, not part of this reward.
3. **How far does the remote work?** Rolling-code RF, roughly 150 ft line of sight as specified by the receiver maker. Each unit's range is measured before shipping and noted on its certificate.
4. **What happens if the remote signal drops or the battery dies?** The control box is wired so that losing the RUN signal kills the ignition, returns the throttle to idle and closes the valve. Manual switches on the pump keep working.
5. **Can someone else's remote start my pump?** The fob uses rolling codes that change every transmission, so a captured signal cannot be replayed. The receivers are paired to your fobs before shipping. (It is not AES-encrypted; we do not claim "encrypted.")
6. **What water source do I need?** Any source the 2 in. suction hose can reach: tank, pond, pool, cistern, creek or irrigation line, with a strainer. Suction lift and hose length reduce flow; we include guidance in the Quick Start Guide.
7. **What are the real flow and pressure numbers?** The pump end is in the 150 GPM / 140 PSI class at 3,600 rpm. Your unit's measured wet-test numbers ship with it.
8. **How long does it run on a tank of fuel?** The iGX390 has a small onboard tank; an auxiliary tank is recommended for long runs. Our design estimate is about ten hours from a 10-gallon auxiliary tank at full throttle. This is an estimate, not a measured figure.
9. **Can I use it while I evacuate?** No. Follow evacuation orders. The remote lets you operate the pump from a distance while you are present; it is not designed for unattended operation.
10. **Will this get me an insurance discount?** We make no claim about discounts or any insurer program. Some insurers and states recognize mitigation; ask your agent.
11. **Is it UL or ETL listed?** No, and there is no UL listing for this product class. The engine carries Honda's EPA/CARB certification; the control box will have an FCC Supplier's Declaration of Conformity.
12. **Is it patented?** Patent pending: two provisional applications filed January 2026 on the automatic remote-start system. The fob-controlled v1 is not itself the subject of those filings.
13. **Why Honda?** The iGX390 has electric start, a drive-by-wire throttle the control box can command, and a nationwide dealer network for engine service and warranty.
14. **Can I retrofit my existing pump?** If it has a Honda iGX390 electric-start engine and a 12 V battery, the Remote Control Kit reward is for you. Other engines are not supported in v1.
15. **Do I need internet, Wi-Fi or a phone?** No. Nothing in v1 connects to anything.
16. **Is there a subscription?** No.
17. **Where do you ship?** Contiguous US only, by LTL freight with liftgate. Pickup in Bend, Oregon, is available.
18. **What is the warranty?** 12 months on the frame, harness, control box, remote system, valve and pump end; Honda's warranty on the engine. Optional 24-month extension add-on.
19. **Is fuel or oil shipped in the unit?** No. Units ship drained. Add oil and fuel per the Quick Start Guide.
20. **What maintenance does it need?** Honda's engine schedule (oil, filter, plug), a fob battery every year or so, and a monthly test run in fire season, which the remote makes easy.
21. **Can I get a refund?** Before parts are ordered (about three weeks after the campaign ends) yes, in full. After that, refunds are at our discretion less non-recoverable costs, and we will always tell you where your unit is in the build.
22. **Do you collect sales tax?** No. Oregon has no sales tax. Backers in other states may owe use tax.
23. **What if the campaign does not reach its goal?** Nobody is charged and nothing is built. That is Kickstarter's all-or-nothing rule.
