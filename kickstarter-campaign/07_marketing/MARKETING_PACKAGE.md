## 6. Press release (draft, unsent)

FOR RELEASE [date]
**Bend inventor puts remote-start wildfire water pump on Kickstarter**
BEND, Ore. — Wildfire Watchdog LLC, a one-person company in Bend, will open a Kickstarter campaign on January 19 for the Dry Lightning 3000, a Honda-powered high-pressure water pump that its owner can start, throttle and open from a handheld key fob rather than standing beside the engine.
Founder Jeremy Coon built the control system in his Bend shop after installing a fixed three-building pump and sprinkler system on the Metolius River in 2026. "Every pump I could buy assumed someone was standing next to it when it mattered," Coon said. "I wanted one I could run from the truck or the end of the driveway, and that would shut itself down safely if the signal or the power dropped."
The pump uses a rolling-code four-button remote wired through relays so that losing power or signal kills the engine, returns the throttle to idle and closes the discharge valve. It has no app, internet connection or subscription. Two provisional patent applications on the automatic version of the system were filed in January 2026.
The campaign seeks $60,000 to build a first batch of ten to thirty-five pumps in Bend, with delivery to the contiguous United States planned for spring and summer 2027. Kickstarter is all-or-nothing: if the goal is not met, no backer is charged.
Coon is careful about what the product is not. "It doesn't detect fire, it doesn't start itself, and it won't make a house fireproof. If you're told to evacuate, evacuate. What it does is let you put water where you need it without walking into the smoke to do it."
