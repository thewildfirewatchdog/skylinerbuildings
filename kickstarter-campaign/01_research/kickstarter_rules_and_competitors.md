# Kickstarter Rules and Competitive Landscape — Dry Lightning 3000

**Product:** Wildfire Watchdog LLC "Dry Lightning 3000" — Honda iGX390 gas-engine high-pressure water pump (~150 GPM / 140 PSI class), rolling-code RF key-fob remote for start / stop / throttle / discharge valve. Built in Bend, Oregon. Target MSRP $5,500. Patent pending (provisional). No app, no internet in v1.
**Research date (access date for every source below):** 2026-09-17
**Prepared by:** research subagent (Claude Code)

## Important note on sourcing

The research environment's egress proxy **blocked direct fetches of every Kickstarter domain** (`www.kickstarter.com`, `help.kickstarter.com`, `updates.kickstarter.com`, `features.kickstarter.com`) and of `web.archive.org`, plus most retailer, news and manufacturer domains (Amazon, Northern Tool, Honda, Waterax, LN Curtis, Brushfire Battle Systems, GeekWire, KQED, OPB, CDI, Oregon DFR, etc.). Every Kickstarter rule below therefore comes from **search-engine extracts of the official Kickstarter pages** (the extract text is quoted where available) cross-checked against secondary sources. The official URL is given in each row so the campaign team can open and re-verify it in a browser before launch. Items marked **[verify]** could not be confirmed from an official extract and must be checked in the live project editor.

## 2. Fee math on a single $5,500 pledge (US project, 2026 published rates)

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gross pledge | — | $5,500.00 |
| Kickstarter platform fee | 5% × $5,500 | −$275.00 |
| Payment processing | 3% × $5,500 + $0.20 | −$165.20 |
| **Net to creator per collected pledge** | | **$5,059.80 (92.0%)** |

Portfolio-level adjustments the financial model should apply:

- **Dropped pledges:** at 3–5% of gross (industry rule of thumb; higher for high-ticket rewards), 100 pledges of $5,500 ($550,000 gross) → $16,500–$27,500 uncollected → 95–97 units actually funded. Net after fees and a 5% drop rate ≈ $480,681 for 100 pledged units (95 collected × $5,059.80).
- **Timing:** payout initiated 14 days after deadline; bank settlement +3–14 business days.
- **Sales tax:** if the Pledge Manager collects sales tax on the reward value for CA/WA/CO backers, that is charged to backers on top, not deducted from the pledge, but it is the creator's compliance obligation.
- **Freight:** not part of the pledge unless bundled; quote separately (crated 3" pump with GX390-class engine typically ships LTL).
- **Late Pledges** are charged the platform's fee schedule as well — **[verify]** current Late Pledge fee on the official page before modelling.

Sources: [Kickstarter Fees: US](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/fees) (accessed 2026-09-17); [PledgeBox: how much does Kickstarter take](https://www.pledgebox.com/post/how-much-does-kickstarter-take) (accessed 2026-09-17); [PledgeBox: dropped backers](https://www.pledgebox.com/post/dropped-backers-how-to-recover-them-to-avoid-funding-loss) (accessed 2026-09-17).

## 7. Positioning implications (10 bullets)

1. **Sell "remote operation," not "protection guarantees."** Kickstarter's prohibited-items list bars items "claiming to prevent a serious injury or death." The safe and truthful pitch is: start, throttle and open the valve from a distance so nobody has to stand at a pump in smoke and embers. Property defense, not life-saving claims.
2. **The working prototype is the campaign.** Every promised function (fob start/stop/throttle/valve, 140 PSI-class output) must be shown on real video. No renders, no CGI fire. Budget for a professional shoot of the real unit at a real pond/pool/cistern in Central Oregon.
3. **Own the price gap explicitly.** A NorthStar GX270 pump is $1,200 and an HPC-2 remote is $300; a DIY stack is ~$1,500–$2,500. The page must itemize what the $5,500 buys beyond that: iGX390 drive-by-wire throttle, rolling-code fob, powered discharge valve, fire-hose fittings, integrated controller, US assembly, warranty and support. Otherwise backers will do the math themselves.
4. **Position between Brushfire Battle Systems ($3.5k–$6.7k carts) and Frontline (~$20k installed).** DL3000 is the "no install, no subscription, no internet, no app" middle tier — a strength given v1 has no connectivity; make that a feature (works when cell/power/internet are down).
5. **Size the goal for a small backer count.** The one direct precedent (Blaze Barrier) got 46 backers; Yarbo M's $4k+ tiers drew 751 with a large brand. Model 15–60 units, not hundreds. A goal around 15–25 units (≈ $80k–$140k gross) is defensible if the pre-launch list is built.
6. **Build the pre-launch list first.** ~31% of pre-launch followers convert and ~29% of funding lands day one. Target fire-safe councils, HOA boards in WUI zones (Bend/Sisters/La Pine, Sierra foothills, Colorado Front Range), rural fire districts, and insurance agents who need mitigation stories for clients.
7. **Lean into insurance pressure without inventing numbers.** Cite the CDI 2026 briefing, Oregon's 2026 mitigation-credit bill and DFR emergency order, and the Bend Source reporting on Deschutes County FAIR Plan growth. Do not claim the DL3000 earns any discount or IBHS designation.
8. **One unit per pledge, kits as add-ons.** Structure rewards as DL3000 (base), DL3000 + hose/nozzle kit, DL3000 + WASP-style sprinkler set — each still one pump, each ≤ $10,000. Offer Bend pickup and a flat US-48 freight tier; skip international for v1.
9. **Front-load risk disclosure.** Risks & Challenges should name Honda engine lead times, RF module FCC status, CARB status for California, absence of UL listing for this product class, provisional (not granted) patent, and a realistic delivery month. Enable Late Pledges before launch so post-deadline wildfire-news demand is captured.
10. **Keep AI out of the assets, or disclose it.** Because disclosed AI use correlates with roughly 40% lower funding in the cited study, and because renders are banned anyway, use real photography/video and human-written copy, then answer the Use of AI questions "no" truthfully.
